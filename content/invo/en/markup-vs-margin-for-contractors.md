# Markup vs margin for contractors: the difference that eats your profit

"I add 20%" sounds like a pricing strategy, but it hides a question: 20% of what? If you mean 20% of your cost, that is markup, and your actual margin is only 16.7%. On a year of jobs, that gap is real money. Here is how to tell the two apart and price on purpose.

## The two formulas

- **Markup** = profit ÷ cost. It answers: "how much did I add on top of what the job cost me?"
- **Margin** = profit ÷ price. It answers: "how much of what the customer paid did I keep?"

Same job, same dollars of profit, two different percentages. Markup is always the larger number.

## Worked example

A job costs you $10,000 in labor, materials, and subs.

- With **25% markup**: $10,000 × 1.25 = **$12,500**. Profit is $2,500.
- Margin on that same job: $2,500 ÷ $12,500 = **20%**.

So "25% markup" and "20% margin" describe the exact same price.

## Markup to margin conversion table

| Markup | Margin |
| --- | --- |
| 10% | 9.1% |
| 15% | 13.0% |
| 20% | 16.7% |
| 25% | 20.0% |
| 33.3% | 25.0% |
| 50% | 33.3% |
| 100% | 50.0% |

To convert yourself: **margin = markup ÷ (1 + markup)** and **markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin)**.

## How to price for a target margin

If you know the margin you need (say 20%) and your cost ($10,000), don't multiply cost by 1.20. Divide instead:

**Price = cost ÷ (1 − target margin)** → $10,000 ÷ 0.80 = **$12,500**.

Multiplying by 1.20 would give $12,000 — a 16.7% margin, and $500 less than you planned.

## Where overhead fits

Markup has to cover more than profit. Insurance, vehicle, phone, software, tools, and the hours you spend estimating and invoicing are all overhead. A simple approach:

1. Add up a year of overhead.
2. Divide by a year of job costs to get your overhead percentage.
3. Add your target profit on top. That total is the margin each job needs to carry.

If overhead is 12% of job costs and you want 10% profit, a 10% markup is not enough.

## Common mistakes

- Treating markup and margin as the same number
- Applying markup to materials but not labor (or the reverse) without meaning to
- Forgetting that discounts come straight out of margin
- Using a markup percentage set years ago, before overhead grew
